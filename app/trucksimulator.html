Fuel Management and Route Planning

Fuel consumption is one of the most overlooked aspects of profitable trucking in the game. Smart players always plan their routes to include fuel stops at strategic locations where prices are lowest, while factoring in the additional distance against potential savings. Monitor your fuel gauge constantly during long hauls, and remember that aggressive driving dramatically increases consumption. Smooth acceleration and maintaining consistent speeds not only save fuel but also reduce wear on your truck components, saving you money on repairs and maintenance.

Cargo Selection and Time Management

Not all delivery contracts are created equal in Truck Simulator. High-paying jobs often come with tight deadlines or challenging routes that can result in penalties if you're not prepared. Always check the delivery distance, time requirements, and potential penalties before accepting contracts. Sometimes a lower-paying job with a generous deadline is more profitable than a high-paying rush delivery that results in speeding fines or late delivery penalties.

Truck Upgrades and Maintenance

Investing in your truck's performance pays dividends over time. Engine upgrades improve fuel efficiency and reliability, while better tires reduce the chance of breakdowns on long hauls. Don't neglect regular maintenance – a well-maintained truck performs better, consumes less fuel, and attracts higher-paying contracts. Schedule maintenance during downtime rather than waiting for emergency repairs that cost significantly more and leave you stranded.

Building Your Reputation
